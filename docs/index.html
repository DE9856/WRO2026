01

Component Roles & Selection (WRO Compliant)

🧠 Computing Layer

🍓
Raspberry Pi 4 Model B — 8 GB
Main single-board computer. Runs the OpenCV vision pipeline and Python state machine. Sends steering angle and speed commands to Arduino via USB serial at 115200 baud.
Quad-core Cortex-A72 @ 1.8 GHz · 8 GB LPDDR4
⚡
Arduino UNO R3 — Real-Time Co-Processor
Handles all time-critical I/O: servo PWM (D9), single stepper drive axis via STEP/DIR/ENABLE (D4, D5, D10), 3x HC-SR04 ultrasonic (round-robin), MPU-6050 over I²C, and start button ISR (D2).
ATmega328P @ 16 MHz · 32 KB Flash · 2 KB SRAM

⚙️ Actuation & Drive — WRO Compliant

🔧
Servo Motor — Steering
Controls Ackermann front steering via mechanical linkage. Driven by D9 at 50 Hz PWM, powered at 6 V from Step-Down #1. Steering range is ±25°.
MG996R · 4.8–7.2 V operating range
🔩
Stepper Motor (STH-39D219) + Belt Drive
Only ONE drive motor is used (WRO 11.5 / 11.13). Shinano Kenshi STH-39D219 NEMA14 stepper, wired bipolar, drives the rear axle through a 20T (motor) → 60T (axle) timing-belt reduction — both rear wheels are mechanically linked on the single belt-driven axle, no electronic differential allowed or used.
1.8°/step (200 steps/rev) · 6-wire, wired bipolar · 20T→60T belt, 3:1 reduction, 6mm belt · 202mm belt length
🎛️
A4988 / DRV8825 — STEP/DIR Driver
Single stepper driver, single motor. Only STEP, DIR, and ENABLE are wired to the Arduino; the motor's center-tap wires are left disconnected (bipolar drive). MS1/MS2/MS3 left at default (full-step, no microstepping). No second driver or motor channel is present on the harness.
Up to ~1–2 A/phase (driver-dependent) · 8–35 V (A4988) / 8.2–45 V (DRV8825) motor input
🛞
Front Wheels — Non-Driven, Ackermann
Two standard rubber wheels (NO caster, NO omni-wheels — WRO 11.4). Free-rotating on a common axle, steered via servo-driven Ackermann linkage.
2× rubber wheels · Ackermann linkage

📡 Sensors

📷
Logitech C270 — USB Webcam
Mounted 15–20 cm from the ground at a 15° downward tilt. Fixed-focus USB UVC webcam (no autofocus hunting), read via OpenCV VideoCapture/V4L2 rather than picamera2. Feeds the OpenCV pipeline for lane detection and pillar identification.
640×480 @ 30 fps (MJPG) · USB 2.0 · CAMERA_FOV/HSV ranges tuned for this camera
📏
HC-SR04 — Ultrasonic Distance ×3
One forward, one left, one right. Front sensor feeds the parking-marker proximity guard (WRO §9.24.7) and triggers a speed reduction when the robot is close to a wall. Left/right are read and logged but not yet fused into corridor centering — see readme.md's "Calibrating for the real robot". Round-robin pinged from firmware so a single loop() never blocks on more than one ping.
2–400 cm range · ±3 mm accuracy · Front: D12/D13 Trig/Echo · Left: D6/D7 · Right: D8/D11
🌀
MPU-6050 — IMU (Gyro + Accel)
Gyro-Z integrated into a yaw-heading estimate (bare I²C register access, no external library). Used by control/parking_maneuver.py for heading-correction during PARK_EXEC, so the vehicle steers to stay parallel to the wall while reversing into the lot (WRO §1.8.2), not just pixel-centered on the markers.
I²C on A4 (SDA) / A5 (SCL) · ±250 °/s gyro range (default) · gyro-only integrator, drifts over long runs
〰️
IR Line Sensor (Analog)
Backup sensor for detecting lap-counting lines on the track floor, in case the camera pipeline misses a line transition at high speed.
Analog output · adjustable sensitivity

🔋 Power System

🔋
Li-Ion / LiPo 7.4 V (2S)
Main power source for the motor rail. Drives the A4988/DRV8825 stepper driver directly. Also feeds both step-down converters.
7.4 V · min 2000 mAh recommended
⬇️
Step-Down #1 — 5 V ≥ 3 A
Logic rail for Raspberry Pi 4B and Arduino UNO. Must supply at least 3 A to avoid brownout on the Pi under heavy CPU/vision load.
Buck converter · 5 V regulated output
⬇️
Step-Down #2 — 6 V ≥ 2 A
Dedicated servo supply. Keeping the servo on a separate rail prevents voltage sag from steering transients affecting the Pi's logic rail.
Buck converter · 6 V regulated output
✓ Rule Compliance — Drive & Steering
WRO 11.4: No caster / ball caster / spherical wheel. Two standard front wheels on Ackermann steering linkage.
WRO 11.5 + 11.13: Single drive motor connected to rear axle. Both rear wheels mechanically linked. Single A4988/DRV8825 STEP/DIR driver, one stepper axis only.

05

Step-by-Step Build (Rule-Compliant)

Chassis, Steering & Belt Drive

Mount two standard front wheels on the Ackermann linkage — no caster wheels. Mount the single stepper motor (STH-39D219) to the chassis and connect it to the rear axle via the 20T (motor) → 60T (axle) timing-belt pulley pair (6 mm belt, 202 mm belt length — a 3:1 reduction) so both rear wheels turn together from the single belt-driven axle. Tension the belt so it can't skip teeth under load/acceleration, but not so tight it loads the motor/axle bearings unnecessarily.

Single-Axis Stepper Wiring

Connect only STEP, DIR, and ENABLE from the Arduino to the A4988/DRV8825 driver, and the motor's four winding leads to the driver's coil outputs (leave the two center-tap wires disconnected — bipolar drive). This ensures only one motor/driver axis is active, which satisfies WRO 11.13.

Top Plate & Sensors

Mount the Raspberry Pi, Logitech C270 (15–20 cm height, 15° downward tilt), Arduino UNO, MPU-6050, 3x HC-SR04 (forward-facing, left-facing, right-facing), and IR sensor to the chassis top plate. Ensure camera has a clear, unobstructed forward view.

Software & Calibration

On the Pi: install opencv-python, picamera2, pyserial. Calibrate HSV colour ranges for each pillar and line colour under your practice lighting. Upload the single-axis stepper-drive Arduino firmware and verify serial communication at 115200 baud.

State Machine

The robot operates through six distinct top-level states (planner/planner.py::State). Transitions are triggered by lap count, sensor events, and image-based detections.

WAIT
→
OPEN_DRIVE / OBS_DRIVE
→ (after 3 laps)
PARK_SEEK
→
PARK_EXEC
→
DONE ✓

Within OPEN_DRIVE/OBS_DRIVE, Planner.lap_phase() reports a lap-based strategy label (not a separate top-level state): lap 1 → MAP_BUILD (cautious), lap 2 → VALIDATE, lap 3+ → SPEED_RUN (aggressive/predictive). See §06A for how this feeds the world model.

State Descriptions
WAIT — Robot is placed in start zone. Holds until start button pressed.
OPEN_DRIVE — Lane-following mode for Open Challenge (no pillars).
OBS_DRIVE — Obstacle Challenge mode: pillar detection active, passes red right, green left.
PARK_SEEK — After 3 laps (24 sections counted), searches for two magenta parking markers.
PARK_EXEC — Executes the parking manoeuvre using MPU yaw feedback.
DONE — Motors stopped. Robot fully inside parking lot.
08

PID Tuning

The PID controller converts the lateral error (from Hough lines or pillar offset) into a servo steering angle. Tune in order: Kp first, then Kd, then Ki.

Kp
0.03 – 0.05
Proportional gain. Controls how aggressively the robot steers toward the target line.
Kd
0.005 – 0.015
Derivative gain. Dampens oscillation, especially on straight sections.
Ki
0.0001 – 0.001
Integral gain. Corrects persistent steady-state offset. Keep small to avoid wind-up.

Confidence-Based Speed Control
Speed dynamically changes according to: confidence, curvature, known section geometry, obstacle certainty, and lap number.

09

Parking Strategy

Detect Magenta Markers

The camera pipeline uses HSV masking (H: 140–170) to detect two magenta markers on either side of the parking lot entrance.

Align Centre

The robot adjusts its lateral position so the midpoint between the two markers is centred in the camera frame.

Drive Past Markers

Robot moves forward until it has fully passed the marker line (the rear axle clears the lot entrance).

Reverse with MPU Feedback

Reverses into the lot using the MPU-6050 yaw delta since PARK_EXEC began (control/parking_maneuver.py) to steer back toward parallel while also centering on the markers. Stops when both pixel-centering and heading are within threshold for several consecutive frames. Wheel-position difference ≤ 2 cm (parallel) is the WRO §1.8.2 scoring check applied by judges at the field, independent of this control loop.

⚠️ Rule 9.24.7 — Markers Cannot Be Touched
The HSV mask is also used to detect proximity to markers. If a marker gets too close to the robot edge, the motor stops immediately to avoid a penalty.
